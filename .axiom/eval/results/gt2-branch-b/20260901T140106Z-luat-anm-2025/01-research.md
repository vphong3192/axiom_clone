## Key findings

**The instrument**
- [B] Law No. 116/2025/QH15 ("Luật An ninh mạng"), 8 chapters. Article count is **disputed
  between sources**: some give 45 articles, one gives 58 (stated as "+15 vs the 2018 law's
  43"). See conflicts. — source: thuvienphapluat.vn, vplsdms.vn, luatvietnam.vn (multiple
  search snippets)
- [B] Passed by the National Assembly on **10 December 2025**, at the 10th session of the
  15th National Assembly, under an **abbreviated/fast-track legislative procedure** (Ministry
  of Public Security still ran a public-comment period 28 June–13 July 2025 on its own portal
  before submission). — source: xaydungchinhsach.chinhphu.vn, thanhnien.vn (MPS proposal
  coverage), thevietnamese.org (fast-track characterization)
- [B] Vote: **434 of 443 delegates present voted in favour, 91.75%** of total NA delegates. —
  source: baochinhphu.vn ("Quốc hội thông qua Luật An ninh mạng (sửa đổi)..."), nhandan.vn
- [B] **Effective date: 1 July 2026** — this is stated consistently across a large majority of
  sources (baochinhphu.vn, congan.ninhbinh.gov.vn, congan.quangtri.gov.vn, luatvietnam.vn,
  frasersvn.com, EY Vietnam). One outlier snippet claimed the law "came into effect on 1
  January 2026" — almost certainly a conflation with the Personal Data Protection Law's
  effective date (see below); flagged in conflicts, not used.
- [B] Replaces/consolidates two prior laws, effective the day the new law takes effect: the
  **Law on Cyber-Information Security No. 86/2015/QH13** (as amended by Law No. 35/2018/QH14)
  and the **Law on Cybersecurity No. 24/2018/QH14**, both of which are repealed. Official
  framing: the new law was "built on the basis of inheriting and consolidating the applicable
  provisions" of the two. — source: thuvienphapluat.vn full-text summary, xaydungchinhsach
  .chinhphu.vn

**What substantively changed**
- [B] **Regulatory concentration**: the Government is affirmed as the unified state manager of
  cybersecurity, with the **Ministry of Public Security (MPS) named as the focal/coordinating
  agency**. This ends the prior split where the Ministry of Information and Communications
  (MIC) handled "information security" (an toàn thông tin mạng) and MPS handled "cybersecurity"
  (an ninh mạng) — a division sources say caused overlapping authority. — source: lawlinkvn.com,
  vplsdms.vn/decuongtuyentruyen.com summaries of the law's chapter on state management
- [B] **Data localisation / foreign presence**: the law requires certain foreign enterprises
  (those meeting criteria to be set by government decree) to establish a branch or
  representative office in Vietnam and to store specified categories of data in Vietnam for a
  government-set period. Multiple explainer sources (VietnamNet "Server có bắt buộc đặt tại
  Việt Nam?", Công an Trà Vinh) explicitly stress the law does **not** mandate that servers
  themselves be located in Vietnam — only that qualifying data be stored domestically, with
  scope and duration left to implementing decrees. This nuance is a common point of confusion.
- [B] **Cross-border data transfer**: the law introduces a mechanism for periodic
  government checks/evaluation of cross-border data transfer activity (not a blanket ban), per
  VietnamNet's "an ninh dữ liệu = tài nguyên quốc gia" framing.
- [B] **Content takedown deadline**: **24 hours** from a formal request by the specialised
  cybersecurity force for organisations/individuals to block, remove, or take down content
  violating Điều 13 (the article listing prohibited content); **shortened to no more than 6
  hours in emergencies threatening national security**. This figure (24h/6h) is repeated
  consistently across VietnamNet, baochinhphu.vn, and a training-institute summary
  (tsqcb.edu.vn), all citing "Điều 13" as the trigger article. — source: vietnamnet.vn ("DN gỡ
  bỏ thông tin vi phạm trong 24 giờ..."), baochinhphu.vn
- [B] **Separately**, English-language secondary sources (Lexology/Mori Hamada legal alerts)
  describe a **24-hour / 3-hour-in-emergency** window for enterprises to *furnish requested user
  information* to authorities. This appears to be a **different obligation** (data/user-info
  disclosure, not content removal) but the two are easy to conflate because both use "24 hours"
  as the baseline and differ only in the emergency figure (6h domestic sources vs 3h in the
  English legal-alert summaries). I could not verify from a primary article number whether this
  is a genuinely separate clause or a garbled restatement of the same one — flagged as an open
  conflict.
- [B] Expanded/clarified list of prohibited content and expanded scope of subjects covered
  (explicitly includes calling for boycotts that damage enterprises, counterfeiting/falsifying
  enterprise information as a new prohibited category) plus new provisions protecting
  "vulnerable groups" online — children, the elderly, people with cognitive difficulties — and
  new provisions on international cooperation against cybercrime. — source: baochinhphu.vn,
  nhandan.vn
- [B] Businesses' obligations shift in character: from "invest in security equipment" toward
  "build cybersecurity governance capability," with sanctions for data-protection failures
  potentially tied to company revenue/incident scale rather than fixed administrative fines
  (this may partly describe the adjacent Personal Data Protection Law rather than this law
  alone — sources blend the two frequently; see conflicts).

**Who is objecting, and on what grounds**
- [B] **Business/industry input (pre-passage)**: VCCI (Vietnam Chamber of Commerce and
  Industry) ran a formal consultation workshop ("Hội thảo góp ý hoàn thiện Dự thảo Luật An
  ninh mạng") gathering business feedback while the bill was in draft. One substantive point
  attributed to businesses in that process: firms argued **they control only a small part of
  cyberspace and should not bear responsibility for the entire space**, given the many other
  actors involved. — source: vibonline.com.vn (VCCI comment archive), vjst.vn coverage. I could
  not find a named VCCI leader or a dated, quotable VCCI statement specifically reacting to the
  final December 2025 text (as opposed to the draft) — treat this as pre-passage input, not a
  post-passage objection.
- [B] I searched specifically for AmCham and EuroCham positions on this law and found **no
  citable statement from either body** in the search results — only generic "raises compliance
  concerns for foreign enterprises" framing from law-firm client alerts (Lexology/Mori Hamada),
  which is the firms' own analysis, not a quoted business-association position. This is a gap,
  not a confirmed absence — flagged below.
- [B] **Human rights / civil-liberties objections**: Human Rights Watch's most clearly sourced,
  quotable statement ("Vietnam: Repeal Harmful Internet Laws," 11 Dec 2024) predates this law —
  it targeted **Decree 147/2024 and the 2018 Cybersecurity Law**, calling on Vietnam to "repeal
  the rights-violating Decree 147 and Law on Cybersecurity, and release everyone imprisoned for
  exercising their free speech rights on the internet." I found **no distinctly dated HRW
  statement issued after the 10 Dec 2025 passage of the new law** in the search results —
  secondary summaries describing "HRW criticism of the 2025 law" appear to be extending the
  2024 statement (about the *predecessor* regime) to the new law by implication, which is
  exactly the kind of attribution trap the brief warns about. Treat the HRW quote as documented
  opposition to the *2018 law and Decree 147*, and the claim that HRW has formally weighed in on
  the *2025 law specifically* as unconfirmed. — source: hrw.org/news/2024/12/11, jurist.org
- [B] Independent Vietnamese-language outlet coverage (The Vietnamese Magazine, "The Quiet
  Passing of Việt Nam's 2025 Cybersecurity Law," Dec 2025) frames the law as a stricter,
  fast-tracked successor to the 2018 law, noting the contrast with the **nationwide public
  protests that met the 2018 law's passage** — this time passed "quietly," without comparable
  public reaction, attributed to the abbreviated procedure and lower public awareness. Same
  outlet has a follow-up piece ("Ambush Lawmaking—Part 3," April 2026) specifically framing
  accelerated/fast-track legislative procedure itself as a civil-society concern. Both pieces
  could not be opened directly (egress blocked); summarized from search snippets only. — source:
  thevietnamese.org (two articles, snippet-only)
- [B] The general critique repeated across rights-focused commentary: prohibited-content
  standards such as "against the State," "inciting division," "causing instability," and
  "threatening security and order" are open-textured/undefined thresholds that **could be
  applied to political advocacy, protest, or sharp policy criticism** at official discretion —
  this is presented as analysis/inference in the search summaries, not as a direct quote from a
  named rights organisation reacting to the 2025 text specifically.

**Government's stated rationale (in its own terms)**
- [B] Institutionalising Party/State policy on protecting national security and sovereignty in
  cyberspace, under unified state management, "while ensuring a balance between cybersecurity
  requirements and socio-economic development and national digital transformation." — source:
  xaydungchinhsach.chinhphu.vn
- [B] Fixing the overlap between MIC (an toàn thông tin mạng) and MPS (an ninh mạng) created by
  having two separate 2015/2018 laws mature side by side for 7–10 years without update. — source:
  congan.camau.gov.vn, soha.vn (MPS deputy minister introducing the law)
  the digital economy — rising data breaches, online fraud, high-tech crime — as the law's
  motivating threat picture. — source: pbgdpl.camau.gov.vn, congan.ninhbinh.gov.vn

**Wider legislative package**
- [B] The **Law on Protection of Personal Data (Luật Bảo vệ dữ liệu cá nhân), No. 91/2025/QH15**,
  was passed 26 June 2025 and took effect **1 January 2026** — passed and effective *before* the
  Cybersecurity Law, not simultaneously with it. Its implementing decree, No. 356/2025/ND-CP, was
  issued 31 December 2025, also effective 1 January 2026. — source: fpf.org issue brief summary,
  recordinglaw.com
- [B] Several Vietnamese-language explainer/commentary pieces (banker.vn, congan.daklak.gov.vn)
  describe the Cybersecurity Law and the Personal Data Protection Law as creating a
  "double-layer" protection framework and speak of the two laws' "coordinated implementation" —
  but I found **no official National Assembly or government statement calling this a bundled
  legislative package**; the "comes bundled with" framing exists **only in commentary/analysis**,
  exactly as the brief anticipated. State this explicitly in the piece rather than implying an
  official package.

## Best approaches considered
- Lead with the passage/effective-date distinction up front, since it is the single most
  commonly muddled fact across sources (one outlier source even misdates the law itself to
  1 January 2026).
- Present the 24h/6h takedown figure as the well-corroborated one (three independent domestic
  outlets, same trigger article), and flag the 24h/3h "user-data furnishing" figure as probably
  a different obligation rather than silently pick one number.
- Keep VCCI's input framed as pre-passage consultation, not post-passage protest — no dated
  post-passage VCCI objection was found.
- Do not assert AmCham/EuroCham took a position; say plainly no citable statement was found.
- Flag the HRW 2024-vs-2025 date gap explicitly — this is the single highest-risk misattribution
  in the topic.

## Pitfalls to avoid
- Do not state "the law replaces the 2018 Law" without also naming the 2015 Law on
  Cyber-Information Security — both are repealed.
- Do not conflate the Cybersecurity Law's 1 July 2026 effective date with the Personal Data
  Protection Law's 1 January 2026 effective date — they are different statutes with different
  effective dates, easily blurred in commentary.
- Do not repeat "58 điều" or "45 điều" as a settled figure without flagging the discrepancy.
- Do not attribute the 2024 HRW "repeal" statement to the 2025 law as if it were issued in
  reaction to the new text.
- Do not present "bundled with the Personal Data Protection Law" as an official framing — it is
  commentary-only.

## Open conflicts / gaps
- **Article count**: 45 vs 58 articles reported for the same law across search snippets of
  ostensibly the same primary text mirror (thuvienphapluat.vn family). Unresolved — could not
  open either page to check page-by-page.
- **Effective date outlier**: one snippet says the Cybersecurity Law "came into effect on
  1 January 2026" against a strong majority (including Chính phủ-linked domains) saying
  1 July 2026. Likely conflation with the Personal Data Protection Law's 1 Jan 2026 date — not
  confirmed as an error, only inferred as one.
- **Takedown window discrepancy**: 24h/6h (content removal, cited to Điều 13, multiple domestic
  outlets) vs 24h/3h (user-information furnishing, English legal-alert summaries). Could not
  confirm from a primary article number whether these are two distinct obligations or one
  obligation reported two ways.
- **HRW and the 2025 law**: no post-10-Dec-2025 HRW statement located; existing HRW quotes
  target the 2018 law and Decree 147. Whether HRW has issued anything specifically about the
  2025 law is an open gap, not a confirmed silence.
- **AmCham / EuroCham**: no citable statement found from either body specifically on this law —
  gap, not evidence of no objection.
- **"Bundled package" framing**: exists only in commentary (banker.vn, congan.daklak.gov.vn
  style pieces); no official source frames the Cybersecurity Law and the Personal Data
  Protection Law as a single legislative package passed together — they were in fact passed six
  months apart (26 June 2025 vs 10 Dec 2025) with different effective dates.
