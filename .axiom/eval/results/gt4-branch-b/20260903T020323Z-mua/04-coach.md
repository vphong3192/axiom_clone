# Self-coach — tản văn "ký ức một cơn mưa"

**Clarity.** Every sentence names a concrete thing doing a concrete thing (the
lamp shrinking and flaring, the clogs going quiet, the puddle spreading). Nothing
hedges an image with a qualifier the way exposition would. No muddy line found.

**Detail.** Objects are named specifically (mái tranh, mái ngói, chõng tre, cửa
liếp, đèn hoa kỳ, guốc mộc, chum sành, quán nước) rather than generalized
("ngôi nhà", "đồ vật") — matches research's instruction directly.

**Completeness.** Covers the full agreed outline: opening on a time adverbial,
the rain's arrival, one object-axis (the lamp/chõng/chum cluster) carrying the
emotional weight, the emptied phố huyện, the rain easing, closing on a static
image. Nothing in the brief's outline is missing, and I did not add scope beyond
it (no dialogue, no named historical event, no moral).

**Alternatives.** A stronger single image was already latent in the draft and I
kept it rather than adding a competing one: the cart-track filling with rain and
dissolving into a spreading puddle (erasure of the day's only visible trace)
is the one candidate for the image that "would actually stop a reader" — it is
not decorative weather, it does the work of showing something disappearing. I
considered opening on it instead of on the sky, but the outline calls for the
rain's arrival to open and a static image to close, and the puddle image reads
better mid-piece, building toward the final drop-and-ring image, than it would
as an opener. Kept the structure as planned.

**Honesty.** No claim is dressed up — this piece asserts nothing that needs
grading, so R2 in its usual sense (evidence dressed up past what it shows) does
not apply. The one place I actively checked for dishonesty of a different kind —
overclaiming distance from the source material — was the borrowing boundary: a
sentence near the end paired "ếch nhái ngoài đồng" with "mùi đất bốc lên" in the
same breath, which is not a copied sentence but does echo the *content* of both
lines research flagged from "Hai đứa trẻ" closely enough to be a real risk, not
just an association any period piece would share. I did not talk myself out of
this by noting the wording differed — wording differing was not the standard the
brief set. I cut the frog and rewrote the sentence around crickets and the smell
of wet earth generally, keeping the sensory beat but removing the specific
combination that made the echo legible.

**Steelman.** Not applicable in the form this checklist usually takes: a tản văn
carries no argument and answers no opposing claim, so there is no position to
steelman and no objection to label as unattributed. The nearest equivalent
question — is there an unfair advantage being taken over the source material —
is the one addressed above under Honesty, and it was worth taking seriously
rather than waving off as out of scope.

**Wow.** Two images carry the piece: the wheel-track dissolving into a widening
puddle, and the closing drop falling from the thatch edge into a ring that then
goes still in the fading light. Both are restrained rather than showy, which is
right for the register — a more startling image would likely have needed either
modern metaphor or overt sentiment, both forbidden here. I judge this true to the
register rather than merely adequate, but I will not claim it clears a bar no one
measured; it is the honest ceiling of what this register allows without breaking
its own rules.

## What I changed after this pass
- Rewrote one sentence (frogs + smell of earth together) to remove the risk of
  echoing the two reference lines from "Hai đứa trẻ" too closely in content,
  replacing "ếch nhái ngoài đồng" with crickets in the roadside grass and
  loosening the earth-smell phrasing further from the flagged line's wording.
- No other line was touched. The net word change from this edit was -1 word
  (43 words removed, 42 added in the replacement clause) — a cut, not a growth,
  consistent with "a repair pass should not grow the work."
