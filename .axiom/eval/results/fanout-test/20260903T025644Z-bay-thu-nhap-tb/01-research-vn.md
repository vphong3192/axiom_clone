## Key findings

- [B] Three different, non-interchangeable "GDP/income per capita" figures are
  in circulation for Vietnam and must not be swapped: (1) nominal GDP per
  capita ≈ **US$5,026** for 2025 (derived from a VND 125.5 million figure,
  domestic reporting); (2) World Bank **Atlas-method GNI per capita** used for
  income classification = **US$4,970** for 2025 (up from US$4,490 in 2024);
  (3) **PPP-adjusted GDP per capita** ≈ **US$14,415** for 2024 (Trading
  Economics/IMF-sourced series). These differ by ~3x between nominal/Atlas and
  PPP — a writer must label which measure is quoted every time. Sources:
  vietnamnet.vn "Vietnam's economy takes off, enters upper-middle income
  bracket"; en.vneconomy.vn "Vietnam officially elevated to upper-middle
  income status"; tradingeconomics.com "gdp per capita ppp" (Vietnam page).

- [B] Approximate PPP GDP-per-capita trajectory (search-aggregated, not from
  a single opened series, so treat as indicative): ~$3,650–3,835 (2000) →
  ~$6,280–6,620 (2010) → ~$11,200–11,800 (2020) → ~$14,415 (2024). Source:
  tradingeconomics.com "gdp per capita ppp" / worldeconomics.com "Vietnam GDP
  Per Capita PPP" page (figures as surfaced in search snippet, not verified
  against the underlying World Bank/IMF table directly).

- [B] World Bank reclassified Vietnam as an **upper-middle-income economy**
  effective with the **1 July 2025 release** (FY2025–26 classification),
  after Atlas GNI per capita rose to $4,970 in 2025 from $4,490 in 2024,
  crossing the **$4,636** lower-middle/upper-middle threshold cited for that
  release. Contributing factors named: 5.9% GDP growth in 2025, exchange-rate
  effects, and — per the World Bank's own account — a revision of population
  estimates as the decisive factor. Sources: en.vneconomy.vn "Vietnam
  officially elevated to upper-middle income status"; vietnam-briefing.com
  "World Bank Reclassifies Vietnam as an Upper-Middle-Income Economy";
  worldbank.org blog "World Bank country classifications by income level for
  2024-2025" (title/URL only, not opened).

- [B] **Trap confirmed live in the data**: the upper-middle threshold moves
  every fiscal year. FY2025–26 lower bound cited alongside Vietnam's
  reclassification is **$4,636**; a separate World Bank blog on FY27
  (1 Jul 2026–30 Jun 2027) gives an updated band of **$4,636–$14,375** (2025
  GNI Atlas basis). A writer using an older figure (e.g., $4,516, seen in
  pre-2024 cycles) for a current-year claim would misstate where Vietnam
  stands. Source: World Bank Data Help Desk "World Bank Country and Lending
  Groups"; worldbank.org blogs "Who moves up and why? ... 2026-2027 ...
  Classifications Release" and "Understanding country income ... for FY26"
  (titles/URLs only, not opened).

- [B] Labour productivity: growth rate has outpaced most ASEAN peers by
  official Vietnamese statistics — GSO-cited average annual growth of ~4%
  (2008–2018) vs. Singapore 0.9%, Malaysia 1.1%, Thailand 2.6%, Philippines
  3.3%, Indonesia 3.4%; separately, 5.1% average for 2011–20 and 4.7% in 2021
  (both described as ASEAN's highest for those periods), and 5.78%/year
  average for 2016–2020. But the **level** remains among the lowest in
  ASEAN: Vietnam's productivity per worker was cited at ~US$12.74 thousand in
  2018, far below Singapore/Brunei. Fast growth from a low base, not
  convergence yet. Sources: vietnamnews.vn "Việt Nam's labour productivity
  needs to catch up with ASEAN"; e.vnexpress.net "Vietnam lags behind ASEAN
  neighbors in labor productivity"; en.vietnamplus.vn "Vietnam's labour
  productivity growth rate fastest in ASEAN" and "Vietnam posts high labour
  productivity growth in ASEAN" (attributed to GSO in the snippets).

- [B] **Party Congress targets — two vintages, do not conflate.** 13th
  National Congress Resolution ("Nghị quyết Đại hội đại biểu toàn quốc lần
  thứ XIII của Đảng"), adopted at the close of the Congress held 25 Jan–1 Feb
  2021 (no separate numbered "số" the way government decisions carry — Party
  congress resolutions are identified by congress ordinal + adoption date):
  targets a developing country with modern industry surpassing lower-middle
  income by 2025; **upper-middle income by 2030** (GDP per capita cited
  around **US$7,500** in some secondary summaries of this resolution); and a
  **developed, high-income country by 2045**. This is the long-cited
  "2030/2045" framing. Sources: vov.vn "Nghị quyết Đại hội XIII xác định 3
  mốc mục tiêu đến năm 2045"; nguoidothi.net.vn "Nghị quyết ĐH XIII: Năm 2045
  là nước phát triển, thu nhập cao"; tulieuvankien.dangcongsan.vn "Những mục
  tiêu phát triển đất nước được thông qua tại Đại hội XIII của Đảng".

- [B] **Newer, superseding target**: the **14th National Congress**, closing
  session **23 January 2026**, adopted a resolution setting **GDP per capita
  of approximately US$8,500 by 2030** (2026–2030 five-year plan), average GDP
  growth ~10%/year, processing-manufacturing ~28% of GDP, digital economy
  ~30% of GDP, HDI ~0.8, life expectancy ~75.5 years. This is described in
  sources as the adopted congress resolution (post-closing), not a draft —
  but note several of the same-titled search hits are draft-document ("Dự
  thảo văn kiện") coverage from before the Congress closed; a writer must use
  only the post-23-Jan-2026 "Nghị quyết Đại hội XIV" reporting, not the draft
  articles, to avoid the "draft treated as adopted" trap — and conversely
  should not keep citing the 13th Congress's $7,500-by-2030 figure as current
  once the 14th Congress's $8,500 figure is in force. Sources: vov.vn "Nghị
  quyết Đại hội XIV: GDP bình quân đầu người đến năm 2030 đạt khoảng 8.500
  USD"; plo.vn "Nghị quyết Đại hội XIV: Phấn đấu thu nhập đầu người đạt 8.500
  USD vào năm 2030"; vnbusiness.vn "Đại hội XIV: Tăng trưởng kinh tế
  10%/năm, GDP 8.500 USD"; contrast with nhadautu.vn "Dự thảo văn kiện Đại
  hội Đảng đặt mục tiêu GDP bình quân đầu người đạt 8.500 USD vào 2030"
  (draft-stage article, same number — suggests the draft and final target
  did not change, but confirm before treating as settled).

- [B] Structural weaknesses, officially/commonly acknowledged in reporting:
  FDI-invested enterprises drove **77.3%** of Vietnam's 2025 goods exports
  (US$367.1bn of US$475.04bn total), i.e., domestic firms account for under a
  quarter of exports. Domestic private firms are described as stuck in
  low-value assembly with limited technology spillover; R&D spending cited at
  only **~0.4% of GDP**. Supporting industries (công nghiệp hỗ trợ) are
  fragmented — many domestic suppliers too small/uncertified to serve FDI
  primes — leaving heavy reliance on imported components/intermediate goods.
  Sources: (export-share figure, publisher/aggregator unclear from snippet —
  see gap below) statista.com "Foreign direct investment (FDI) into Vietnam –
  statistics & facts"; globalfdiinsights.com "Vietnam Supporting Industries:
  Driving Industrial Growth & Supply Chain Transformation"; researchgate.net
  "(PDF) The paradox of FDI in Vietnam"; ash.harvard.edu "Vietnam's
  Industrial Policy" (Ash Center working paper, title/URL only, not opened).

## Pitfalls to avoid

- Do not quote nominal (~$5,026), Atlas/GNI (~$4,970), and PPP (~$14,415)
  figures interchangeably — pick the one that matches the claim (income
  classification = Atlas GNI; standard-of-living comparisons = PPP).
- Do not cite the 13th Congress's $7,500-by-2030 figure without noting the
  14th Congress (Jan 2026) revised the 2030 GDP-per-capita target to ~$8,500
  — using the older number as "the" current target would be stale, not
  wrong-per-se, but should be flagged as superseded.
- Do not treat pre-23-Jan-2026 "dự thảo" (draft) Đại hội XIV coverage as
  adopted policy; use only post-closing "Nghị quyết" reporting.
- Do not apply an outdated World Bank income threshold (e.g., $4,516) to a
  2025/2026 classification claim; the FY26/FY27 lower bound is $4,636.
