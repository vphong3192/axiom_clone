## Key findings

### The 2017–2020 solar/wind boom and its unwind (2022–2024 inspections)

- **[B]** In 2023, Vietnam's Government Inspectorate (Thanh tra Chính phủ) issued a
  conclusion finding that the Ministry of Industry and Trade (MOIT) had approved/added
  solar power projects to the power development scheme (PDP7-revised) without adequate
  legal basis, causing source-grid imbalance and, per the Inspectorate's own language,
  a lack of transparency in solar supplementation "with risk of quid-pro-quo practices"
  (nguy cơ xin–cho). — source: WebSearch synthesis of vietnamnet.vn, daidoanket.vn,
  lsvn.vn, nld.com.vn coverage of the Thanh tra Chính phủ kết luận (Dec 2023).
- **[C — conflicting/unverified figures, do not cite as precise]** Different search
  results returned **inconsistent counts** for how many solar projects/how much capacity
  were found to lack legal planning basis: one synthesis cites "154 projects... 123
  identified as primary cause"; another cites "54 projects/10,521 MW without legal
  planning basis"; a third cites "168 projects/14,707 MW (17.3× the 2020 target)" with
  "129 projects/8,642 MW" already operating commercially. These may refer to different
  sub-scopes (different inspection periods, or PDP7 vs PDP7-revised) but I could not
  open the actual Thanh tra Chính phủ conclusion text to reconcile them. **Do not use
  any single one of these numbers as if it were confirmed** — flag the scale ("well
  over 100 unplanned/irregularly-approved solar projects") as the only safely reportable
  claim, at B.
- **[C]** EVN reportedly overpaid roughly VND 1,481 billion (~$62m) from 2020 through
  mid-2023 due to projects receiving incorrect preferential (FIT) rates — single-sourced
  figure via WebSearch synthesis, not independently opened. Needs confirmation before use.
- **[B]** Separately, MOIT/EVN reporting identified **173 solar and wind plants (or
  plant-units)** nationwide that reached commercial operation date (COD) and/or received
  FIT1/FIT2 preferential prices **without** the required construction-completion
  acceptance (CCA) sign-off from competent authorities at the time — a systemic
  procedural gap, not a single scandal. — source: WebSearch synthesis of tuoitre.vn,
  baochinhphu.vn, nld.com.vn reporting on FIT/COD irregularities (2023–2025 coverage).
- **[B]** From June 2023, MOIT issued a new circular requiring renewable-energy projects
  to hold a CCA certificate *before* COD can be recognized — effectively disqualifying
  affected projects from claiming the original high FIT rates going forward. — source:
  WebSearch synthesis of reccessary.com / theinvestor.vn coverage.
- **[B]** Some specific rooftop/utility-scale projects publicly named as having received
  FIT prices "for the wrong category" (không đúng đối tượng) include Hacom Solar,
  Sinenergy Ninh Thuận 1, Thuận Nam Đức Long, Thiên Tân Solar Ninh Thuận, and Phước Ninh —
  source: WebSearch synthesis of tuoitre.vn coverage, Dec 2023. One example cited:
  the Thuận Nam Đức Long project reportedly went from construction start (~mid-Sept 2019)
  to grid connection in about 3.5 months, in order to make the 31 Dec 2019/2020 FIT
  cutoff — **[C]**, single-sourced, timeline plausible but unverified in a primary
  document.
- **Gap/limitation:** I could not find, via WebSearch, any case specifically and
  distinctly framed as "backdated **rooftop** (mái nhà) solar fraud" separate from the
  broader utility-scale COD/FIT-eligibility problem described above. The intake brief
  names this as a target; what I found is the general COD/CCA-backdating problem across
  both rooftop and utility-scale segments, not a rooftop-specific prosecuted fraud ring.
  This should be flagged as an open gap for the writer, not filled with a guess.

### Criminal prosecutions tied to the solar boom (2024–2025)

- **[B]** On 2 January 2024, the Security Investigation Agency of the Ministry of Public
  Security prosecuted and arrested **Hoàng Quốc Vượng** (b. 1963), former Deputy Minister
  of Industry and Trade (also a former Chairman of EVN and a former Chairman of PVN), on
  charges of "abusing position and power while performing official duties"
  (lợi dụng chức vụ, quyền hạn trong khi thi hành công vụ). — source: WebSearch synthesis
  of baochinhphu.vn (government portal), vietnamnet.vn, tienphong.vn, Jan 2024.
- **[B]** The core allegation: while Deputy Minister, Vượng is accused of directing MOIT's
  Electricity & Renewable Energy Department to draft Decision 13/2020 in a way that
  expanded FIT eligibility, and of personally approving a preferential price of 9.35
  US cents/kWh for the Trung Nam – Thuận Nam solar plant (Ninh Thuận). — source:
  WebSearch synthesis of vietnamnews.vn (VNA's English service) reporting on the trial.
- **[B]** Prosecutors allege Vượng received VND 1.5 billion (~US$57,900) from Nguyễn Tâm
  Thịnh, chairman of the Trung Nam – Thuận Nam solar company board, as a bribe connected
  to this decision. — source: WebSearch synthesis of vietnamnews.vn trial coverage;
  single English-language state-linked source, not cross-verified against a second outlet
  in this session — treat as B but flag limited sourcing.
- **[C]** Damage-to-EVN figures cited for this case vary by scope and are single-sourced
  in this session: "over VND 937 billion" (as of 28 June 2023, apparently specific to the
  Trung Nam–Thuận Nam project) vs. "over VND 4 trillion" (EVN's total 2020–2024
  overpayment across "two solar power plants" per one source) vs. "VND 1.48 trillion"
  (EVN's 2020–mid-2022 overpayment across 14 Ninh Thuận projects, per another source).
  These are plausibly non-overlapping scopes (one project vs. two plants vs. 14 projects)
  but I cannot reconcile or confirm any of them without the primary indictment/inspection
  document — cap all at C, present only as "hundreds of billions to several trillion VND
  in disputed FIT overpayments across multiple projects," not as precise figures.
- **[B]** Hoàng Quốc Vượng and at least 10 co-defendants (including former Electricity &
  Renewable Energy Department director general Phương Hoàng Kim) went to trial in Hà Nội;
  prosecutors reportedly sought 6–7 years' imprisonment for Vượng, and he was **sentenced
  to 6 years** (late April 2025). — source: WebSearch synthesis of vnexpress.net and
  tuoitre.vn trial/sentencing coverage, April 2025.
- **[B]** In August 2024, the Ministry of Public Security's Security Investigation Agency
  requested EVN provide full case files on **32 wind and solar projects** (10 solar, 22
  wind) across multiple provinces — concentrated in the Central, Central Highlands and
  Mekong Delta regions — as part of the same "abuse of position and power" investigation
  into MOIT and provincial authorities. The Trung Nam – Thuận Nam plant (450 MW,
  Trungnam Group) was named as the largest project on the list. — source: WebSearch
  synthesis of baochinhphu.vn (govt policy portal), cand.com.vn (Ministry of Public
  Security's own newspaper), dantri.com.vn, nld.com.vn, Aug 2024. The 450 MW capacity
  figure is corroborated across outlets (B); the cited VND 12,000 billion construction
  cost for that plant is single-sourced in this session (C).
- **[B]** Separately and more recently — **July 2026** — Vietnam's police prosecuted **47
  defendants** in a case at the National Power Transmission Corporation (EVNNPT, EVN's
  transmission subsidiary), tied to the 500kV "mạch 3" (circuit 3) transmission line
  project, on charges spanning bidding violations, accounting violations, embezzlement,
  and bribery (giving and receiving). Authorities reported freezing/seizing roughly
  VND 1,756 billion and over US$1 million. — source: WebSearch synthesis of vnexpress.net,
  vietnamplus.vn, tuoitre.vn, dantri.com.vn, vietstock.vn, all dated 2 July 2026. Note:
  this case concerns **transmission infrastructure**, not renewable generation directly —
  relevant as evidence the energy-sector anti-corruption drive continues into 2026 under
  Tô Lâm, but should not be conflated with the solar/wind-specific cases above.

### Party-level accountability (distinct from criminal prosecution — do not conflate)

- **[B]** In December 2023 (Kỳ họp thứ 34), the Party's Central Inspection Commission
  (Ủy ban Kiểm tra Trung ương — a **Party disciplinary organ**, not a court or the
  National Assembly) concluded that senior officials including **Trần Tuấn Anh**
  (Politburo member, Head of the Central Economic Commission, former Minister of
  Industry and Trade 2016–2021) and **Trịnh Đình Dũng** (former Deputy PM) bore
  responsibility for violations in advising on/issuing solar and wind power development
  mechanisms and in implementing the revised Power Master Plan VII (PDP7-revised), among
  other issues (fuel trading licensing, price stabilization fund management). — source:
  WebSearch synthesis of tuoitre.vn, vietnamnet.vn, RFA Vietnamese, baogiaothong.vn,
  Dec 2023–Jan 2024 coverage.
- **[B]** In January 2024 (Kỳ họp thứ 35, 10–11 & 19 Jan), the Central Inspection
  Commission formally **proposed** that the Politburo and Secretariat discipline Trần
  Tuấn Anh; a warning-level discipline (cảnh cáo) was separately imposed on several other
  MOIT officials. — source: WebSearch synthesis of thanhtra.com.vn, vietstock.vn,
  vietnambiz.vn, Jan 2024.
- **[B]** On 26 January 2024, the Party Central Committee approved Trần Tuấn Anh's
  request to step down from the Politburo and Central Committee (Party bodies — this is
  a Party-level removal, distinct from any criminal charge; I found no evidence in this
  session that Trần Tuấn Anh has been criminally charged). — source: WebSearch synthesis
  of tuoitre.vn, 26 Jan 2024.
- **Precision flag:** these are **Party disciplinary actions** ("đề nghị kỷ luật",
  "cảnh cáo", removal from Politburo), issued by the Central Inspection Commission /
  Politburo — a *Party* tier, separate from the *state* criminal-justice process that
  produced the Hoàng Quốc Vượng conviction. Keep these two tracks distinct in the
  write-up; conflating "kỷ luật Đảng" with "bị kết án" would be a factual error even
  though both concern the same underlying solar/wind policy episode.

### EVN's structural position and market-liberalization moves

- **[B]** EVN remains the dominant state-owned buyer of electricity and holds a
  monopoly on transmission and distribution in Vietnam. — source: WebSearch synthesis of
  IEEFA and industry-briefing coverage of the DPPA reform.
- **[B]** Decree 80/2024/NĐ-CP (issued July 2024) established Vietnam's Direct Power
  Purchase Agreement (DPPA) mechanism, which for the first time allows qualifying large
  consumers (≥200,000 kWh/month average) to contract directly with renewable generators
  (≥10 MW, solar/wind) via a "virtual" grid-mediated model, or via genuinely private
  wire in the fully-private model — a partial break from EVN's monopoly position, after
  roughly seven years of advocacy (including from foreign business chambers such as
  EuroCham) and reportedly ~15 draft versions. — source: WebSearch synthesis of IEEFA,
  WRI, S&P Global, Vietnam Briefing, Duane Morris coverage, 2024–2025.
- **[B]** EVN carried substantial accumulated financial losses through 2023–2024; MOIT
  in 2025 proposed passing roughly VND 45 trillion (~US$1.8bn) of EVN's accumulated
  losses into future retail tariffs, with retail prices raised multiple times
  (e.g., +3% May 2023, +4.5% Nov 2023, +4.8% May 2025) against a backdrop of Hanoi
  historically keeping power prices politically low to protect inflation/exports/social
  stability. — source: WebSearch synthesis of GG Power, Finimize, vietnamnews.vn,
  Vietnam Policy Studies newsletter, and an Asia Times analysis piece ("Vietnam's power
  bill hides a market economy lie," 2026), 2023–2026 coverage. The specific VND 38.68
  trillion "accumulated loss at end-2024" figure is single-sourced in this session — **[C]**.

### Party strategic framework predating the current "green wave"

- **[B]** Politburo Resolution 55-NQ/TW, signed 11 February 2020 by then General
  Secretary/President Nguyễn Phú Trọng, set the national energy strategy orientation to
  2030/2045, including targets for renewables to reach ~15–20% of primary energy supply
  by 2030 and ~25–30% by 2045, and explicitly called for "breakthrough" mechanisms/special
  policies to fast-track priority power-source investment projects. — source: WebSearch
  synthesis of the Politburo document as reproduced on tinhuykhanhhoa.vn (provincial
  Party portal) and moit.gov.vn. This is a **Party Politburo resolution** (issuing level:
  Đảng, not National Assembly/government) — important because it shows the political
  mandate for rapid renewable build-out was set in 2020, *before* the 2023–2024
  inspection/prosecution wave, and its own "fast-track mechanism" language is plausibly
  part of what created the approval shortcuts later flagged as irregular.

### Leadership turnover 2023–2025 and bearing on policy continuity

- **[B]** President Nguyễn Xuân Phúc was forced to resign in January 2023, taking
  political responsibility for two corruption scandals (Việt Á COVID test-kit case and
  the repatriation-flight bribery case) that occurred during his tenure; two deputy PMs
  (Phạm Bình Minh, Vũ Đức Đam) were also removed. — source: WebSearch synthesis of
  RFA English, ISEAS Perspective 2023/41 (Nguyễn Khắc Giang), Jan 2023.
  His successor, Võ Văn Thưởng, was elected President in March 2023 and himself
  resigned in March 2024, roughly a year later, again amid the anti-corruption campaign
  — source: WebSearch synthesis of NPR, Al Jazeera, CNN, GPB, March 2024.
- **[B]** Following General Secretary Nguyễn Phú Trọng's death (July 2024), Tô Lâm
  (former Minister of Public Security, 2016–2024) was elected General Secretary on 3
  August 2024, holding both the General Secretary and State President titles
  concurrently until Lương Cường was elected President on 21 October 2024 (440/440
  votes at the 15th National Assembly's 8th session). — source: WebSearch synthesis of
  Vietnamese Wikipedia leadership timeline cross-checked against baochinhphu.vn and
  plo.vn coverage.
- **[B — reasoned, from ISEAS scholarship]** Analyst Nguyễn Khắc Giang (ISEAS – Yusof
  Ishak Institute) argues the anti-corruption campaign, while formally an integrity
  drive, has simultaneously (a) broken up some entrenched interest groups and reduced
  informal costs of doing business, while (b) slowing public investment disbursement and
  bureaucratic decision-making (citing, e.g., Q1 2023 disbursement rates under 5% at
  >30% of localities and 44 central agencies with zero disbursement), and (c) reshaping
  central-local power relations and elite succession politics ahead of the 14th Party
  Congress (2026). — source: WebSearch synthesis of ISEAS Perspective 2023/41 and
  2024/44 by Nguyễn Khắc Giang, plus his 2026/29 piece on the post-Congress leadership
  reconfiguration (titles/dates confirmed via ISEAS's own listing; content is search-tool
  summary, not read directly — cap at B, treat as a reputable secondary/scholarly source
  per the honesty rules).
- **[B]** Under Tô Lâm as General Secretary, the anti-corruption apparatus (Ban Chỉ đạo
  Trung ương về phòng, chống tham nhũng, lãng phí, tiêu cực) has explicitly named energy
  and environment-linked cases (citing, e.g., the National Power Transmission
  Corporation and Long Thành airport) among the "large, serious, complex" cases being
  pushed to resolution — indicating the energy sector remains a live target under the
  new leadership, not a closed chapter. — source: WebSearch synthesis of
  baochinhphu.vn / xaydungchinhsach.chinhphu.vn coverage of Tô Lâm's chairing of
  Steering Committee meetings, 2024–2026.
- **[B — reasoned]** Commentary from Fulcrum (ISEAS) and CFR (US think tank) frames Tô
  Lâm's 2024–2026 trajectory as one of significant power consolidation (institutional
  restructuring, provincial mergers, cadre rotation) running in parallel with continued
  anti-corruption enforcement — supporting a reading that anti-graft enforcement (including
  the energy-sector cases above) has been, among other things, an instrument of political
  consolidation, not solely a technocratic clean-up. I was not able to open the Fulcrum
  or CFR pieces directly this session (403); this is a WebSearch-snippet-level synthesis
  of their argument, cap at B and attribute the interpretation to named outlets, not to
  this research file as fact.

### External pressure intersecting with domestic politics (JETP)

- **[B]** Vietnam's Just Energy Transition Partnership (JETP), agreed December 2022,
  pledged US$15.5 billion (split roughly $7.75bn International Partners Group / $7.75bn
  GFANZ private finance), with 8 priority projects selected in 2024 but — per UNDP's
  Vietnam resident representative — no funds yet disbursed as of the most recent
  reporting found. — source: WebSearch synthesis of Rockefeller Foundation JETP report,
  IISD "Just Energy Transition in the Media," EEAS coverage, 2024–2025.
- **[B]** In March 2025 the US Treasury announced it was withdrawing from the JETP
  arrangements with Vietnam, Indonesia and South Africa, putting the ~$45bn combined
  initiative "in limbo." — source: WebSearch synthesis of DevelopmentAid.org coverage,
  March 2025.
- **[B — reasoned/opinion, attribute to source]** The Heinrich Böll Foundation (a German
  think tank) has argued the JETP carries "high political costs": during the 2021
  negotiation period, four prominent Vietnamese civil-society figures working on
  environmental/land-rights/energy-policy issues were arrested (officially on tax-evasion
  grounds) and given multi-year sentences in 2022, which the Foundation reads as a signal
  that international donors are tolerating civil-society repression in exchange for
  climate cooperation. This is one advocacy organization's interpretation, not a
  proven causal claim — present as attributed opinion/analysis, not as fact about
  government motive.

## Pitfalls to avoid

- **Don't conflate Party discipline with criminal conviction.** "Đề nghị kỷ luật" /
  "cảnh cáo" (Central Inspection Commission recommending or imposing Party discipline on
  Trần Tuấn Anh et al.) is a different track, with a different evidentiary bar, from
  Hoàng Quốc Vượng's criminal prosecution and 6-year sentence. The write-up should never
  say Trần Tuấn Anh was "convicted" or "arrested" — that is not supported by anything
  found this session.
- **Don't present the conflicting solar-project counts (154/123, 54/10,521MW,
  168/14,707MW, 129/8,642MW) as if they were a single reconciled figure.** They likely
  come from different inspection scopes/periods; without the primary document I cannot
  merge them. Report only the qualitative scale ("well over 100 projects added without
  adequate legal/planning basis during the 2017–2020 boom").
- **Don't treat "backdated rooftop solar fraud" as a confirmed, distinctly-prosecuted
  phenomenon** — what is documented is a broader COD/FIT-eligibility backdating problem
  spanning both utility-scale and (per MOIT's 173-plant figure) some rooftop-adjacent
  cases; I found no discrete "rooftop solar fraud ring" prosecution distinct from this.
- **Don't collapse "the current (2024–2026) green-transition acceleration" with
  "the 2017–2020 solar boom that is now being prosecuted."** The corruption cases concern
  decisions made largely 2017–2021 (Decision 11/2017, Decision 13/2020, PDP7-revised);
  the prosecutions are a retrospective clean-up running 2023–2026. Evidence found this
  session supports treating the anti-corruption wave as (a) punishment/clean-up of a
  *prior* boom and (b) an instrument of *political consolidation* under Tô Lâm — it is
  weaker evidence, on what I found, for the claim that *today's* renewable push is itself
  primarily a patronage vehicle. The design/writer should weigh this distinction
  carefully rather than merging the two eras.
- **Don't cite the Heinrich Böll "political costs" framing as established government
  motive** — it is one advocacy think tank's interpretation of a pattern (arrests of
  environmental/civil-society figures around the JETP negotiation), not a proven causal
  chain connecting JETP funding to those arrests.
